Cohort module five, two hours

Build Self-Healing Engineering Loops

Recovering from a failed check without a human restarting anything, and knowing when to give up. The second half is about stopping, which is the part people skip.

0:00 to 0:35

Handing back a reason, not a rejection

Goal

A failed check produces a next attempt that starts from what went wrong.

Say

  • Show the useless version first: a failure returned as a status with no detail, and the retry that repeats the same mistake.
  • Build the useful shape together. What failed, the output, the file, and what the task said should have been true.
  • Be clear about why the roles are split. An agent grading its own output has already invested its reasoning in defending it.

Run

$ node steps/05-loop/demo.ts run 21

The loop is steps/05-loop/loop.ts.

Room does

  • Watches a first attempt fail, and reads the packet that goes back.
  • Watches the second attempt pass, having been told precisely what was wrong.

Ask

What does your pipeline hand back today when something fails, and would it help anyone?

0:35 to 1:10

Three ways to end

Goal

Nobody leaves with a loop that can run indefinitely.

Say

  • Ask what happens to their loop when the task is impossible. The honest answer is usually that nobody has tried.
  • Name the three endings and make them choose numbers for their own project rather than copying the lab's.
  • Reframe giving up. An item returned to a person with its attempts recorded is a result; a run still going is not.

Run

$ node steps/05-loop/demo.ts run 22 --impossible

Room does

  • Sees attempts exhausted and the item escalated with its history intact.
  • Sets an attempt limit and a time limit in their own configuration.

Ask

What is the most you would spend on one item before a person should look at it?

1:10 to 1:45

Where the time went

Goal

Everyone is timing each stage separately and can see which one to attack.

Say

  • Ask them to guess the split before showing it. The guesses are almost always wrong in the same direction.
  • Show the recorded numbers and let the surprise do the work, then name the three usual culprits: setup repeated per attempt, checking that repeats work already done, and waiting on a person with nothing saying so.
  • Give the actionable version. Reuse caches across workspaces, let the reviewer consume evidence rather than regenerate it, and make waiting visible.

Run

$ node steps/05-loop/demo.ts budget

Room does

  • Reads a waterfall of stages for one completed item.
  • Names the stage they would reduce first, and says how.

Ask

Which stage would you attack first on your own project, and what evidence do you have?

1:45 to 2:00

Capstone scope

Goal

Everyone has chosen the real item from their own backlog that they will demonstrate.

Say

  • Give the selection rule: small enough to finish, real enough that somebody wanted it, and touching more than one area if possible.
  • Warn against the demonstration-shaped item. Choosing something invented for the occasion wastes the exercise.

Run

Nothing. Backlogs open, choices written in the shared document.

Room does

Commits to one item by name and posts it where the group can see.

Ask

Is anyone's choice too large? Say it now rather than on demonstration day.

1:35 to 1:50

Coming back from red

Goal

They can distinguish a retry that uses the reason from one that merely repeats.

Say

  • A loop that retries without reading the failure is a slower way to fail the same way.
  • Compare the two attempts and find the sentence in the second that only exists because of the first.
  • Cap the attempts out loud: twice with the reason attached, then a person.

Run

$ make score

Room does

  • Finds the failure text carried into the second attempt.
  • Sets a retry limit for their own repository and writes the reason beside it.

Ask

How many times should something fail before the failure becomes yours rather than its?

Concepts: two roles, how a loop ends, the time budget.
