Cohort module six, two hours

Orchestrate and Ship Verified PRs

Everything connects. The last half hour is the build against buy conversation, answered by mapping what they built onto a hosted product.

0:00 to 0:45

One workflow, six layers

Goal

Each person's pieces run as a single command against a real item in their own repository.

Say

  • Resist rebuilding anything. This session connects what exists, and the connecting code should be short and boring.
  • Walk the seams rather than the parts: what triage hands to implementation, what implementation hands to checking, what checking hands to delivery.
  • Point out that every handoff is written somewhere durable, so a run that dies can be picked up rather than restarted.

Run

$ make demo STEP=06

Room does

  • Runs the whole sequence on their own item, unattended.
  • Reads their own handoff records and confirms each stage could be resumed.

Ask

Kill your run halfway. Can the next one continue from what it left behind?

0:45 to 1:15

What a reviewer receives

Goal

Their requests arrive with enough attached that a colleague could judge one in a few minutes.

Say

  • Have them review each other's output rather than their own. Reviewing your own factory tells you nothing.
  • Collect what reviewers wanted and did not find, and add those to the template on the spot.
  • Be firm about the boundary. Nothing merges itself at any tier, and the enforcement belongs in the repository settings rather than in a script.

Run

$ node steps/06-delivery/demo.ts open-pr
$ node steps/06-delivery/demo.ts try-merge

Room does

  • Reviews a peer's request and reports what was missing.
  • Watches an attempted merge blocked, and checks their own protection settings.

Ask

What did your partner need that your template did not give them?

1:15 to 1:45

The same thing, bought

Goal

Everyone can decide for their own organisation, with reasons that are not about preference.

Say

  • Open the hosted product and map its stages onto their own, out loud, one at a time. The correspondence is close and that is the lesson.
  • Show where their policy lands on the other substrate: a function that permits or refuses a move, holding the same rules they wrote in week one.
  • Frame the decision honestly. This is about who maintains the parts you now understand, not about whether to have them.

Run

$ make demo STEP=07

The mapping is written out in steps/07-mastra/README.md.

Room does

  • Sees a board whose columns are the stages they built by hand.
  • Sees their protected path expressed as a policy function on the other substrate.

Ask

Which layers would you hand to a vendor, and which would you never give up?

1:45 to 2:00

Demonstration day

Goal

Everyone knows the shape of what they will show and how long they have.

Say

  • Give the running order and the timing, and say what you will ask each of them.
  • Set the expectation that a refusal or an escalation is a better demonstration than a clean pass, because it shows the limits working.

Run

Nothing. Schedule on screen.

Room does

Picks a slot and confirms their item still runs end to end.

Ask

Anyone whose item stopped working this week? Bring it to Friday instead of debugging alone.

1:35 to 1:50

Finished, and still not merged

Goal

They stop treating a board reaching done as a claim that anything shipped.

Say

  • Done on the board means the machine has nothing left to do, which is not the same as merged.
  • Two boards, and this is the one for issues; the pull request lives on the other with its own phases.
  • Anyone who conflates them will report a week of work that is still sitting unreviewed.

Run

$ make e2e

Room does

  • Finds the same change represented on both boards.
  • Writes the sentence they would use in a status update, and has it corrected.

Ask

Your dashboard says twelve done. How many of those can a customer use?

Concepts: durable state, the delivered request, who decides, the record, stages on a board, policy that stays yours.
